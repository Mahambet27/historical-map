# Historical data verification

## Roles

- Editors prepare draft text, media and geometry.
- Historians review claims, chronology, uncertainty and source quality.
- Administrators publish a reviewed, internally consistent release.
- Anonymous visitors have read-only access to published or verified material.

## Evidence rules

Prefer peer-reviewed books and articles, archival catalogues, archaeological reports, museum
collections, official legal portals and established encyclopedias. Oral history is accepted when
its provenance and consent are recorded. Wikipedia may be an additional orientation link, never
the sole source for a key claim.

Every entity, event, person and geometry must have a source link and review status. Approximate
dates use an explicit precision. Geometry has a separate validity range, version, method and
confidence level (`high`, `medium`, `low`, `disputed`).

## Borders and disputed interpretations

Reviewers compare the map against its cited evidence and record what the polygon represents:
settlement, influence, claimed territory or administrative border. Alternative interpretations
are stored as separate versions. A disputed version is never silently merged into a single
“official” outline. Visitor-facing text always states that historical boundaries are scholarly
reconstructions.

## Publication

1. Editor creates a draft and links sources.
2. Automated validation checks years, coordinates, geometry and missing translations.
3. A historian records a review and any disagreement.
4. An administrator publishes one atomic data release.
5. Material is versioned; later corrections create a new review rather than overwriting evidence.
